00Assumptions, context and what I decided
Where this came from
No codebase, UI kit or design system was attached, so the brief is the only design context — and you asked me to treat it as law. Every parameter below is either quoted from it or a decision I made because it said the decision was mine. Nothing here is invented for decoration.
The four inspirations named in the brief are read as follows: the card rip takes its gesture contract and card-by-card choreography from mobile TCG pack openers; the drop takes its scarcity energy from live-rip streams; portfolio and marketplace take their density and P&L legibility from brokerage and premium-collectible apps. None of their UI is copied — the patterns are, the surfaces are original.
These are mockups, not the React Native build. The reveal is shown as annotated storyboards plus one interactive gesture stand-in — I can't render real 3D or generate imagery here, so every pack, card and watch is a striped or gradient placeholder for real assets.
The system
One luxury shell, two category accents, per your call. Shell: near-black warm grounds (#0b0a09 / #0e0d0b), bone type (#EDE7DA), Instrument Serif for money and object names, Archivo for interface, JetBrains Mono for anything numeric or diagnostic. Cards accent oklch(.74 .13 262); watches accent oklch(.74 .13 78) — identical lightness and chroma, hue apart, so neither category outranks the other.
Tier is expressed by how much room a SKU gets, not by badges: $10 packs are half-width chips, the $2,000 box breaks the gutter and goes full-bleed, the $5,000 vault gets a plate of its own. Drops drop the chrome entirely.
Motion split, held everywhere: cards use ease-out-expo with overshoot and spend their haptic budget on frequency. Watches use cubic-bezier(.22,.61,.36,1), never overshoot, and spend their budget on silence.

2Pulled closer to the four references — original surfaces, their patterns
2aCard rip, mobile-TCG-opener grade. Swipe up on the wrapper, then swipe left through the cards. What changed from 1e: the reveal is full-bleed with zero chrome, the tear is a vertical swipe on a wrapper that peels rather than a foil that slides, cards come off a stack one at a time as a fan you push through, and the pip row is replaced by a stack-depth indicator so you always feel how many are left. Rare cards arrive with light rays and refuse to advance for 1.6s.

What the gesture owes you
Wrapper peel: vertical, 1:1, 0→1 over 200px. Under 55% travel and under 850px/s it falls back closed. The wrapper never animates on tap-up — only on release.
Card advance: horizontal swipe, the top card tracks the thumb with 0.4° rotation per 10px, throws off past 90px or 600px/s, otherwise snaps back. Stack depth stays visible behind it.
Rare gate: the chase card sets canAdvance=false for 1.6s. Swipes during the gate are absorbed, not queued — you cannot outrun the moment, and it never feels like input was dropped because the card leans toward the thumb.
Rays, bloom and sheen are all one additive material on a plane behind the card, driven by a single rarity float 0→1. Nothing about the rare path is a separate scene.
2bDrop, with live-rip energy. What changed from 1d: the countdown is no longer the whole screen. A live activity feed runs under it — real claims, named, timestamped, arriving as they happen — so scarcity is something you watch other people consume rather than a number that decrements quietly. The unit counter and the feed are the same event stream.
